# Getting started

## Install and set up

Open `StableBar.dmg`, drag StableBar to Applications, then open the app. Setup opens over your whole screen with a short intro: your wallpaper fades in and settles from a slight zoom, then dims and softens as StableBar's name rises under a sweep of light; a warm line widens from the middle, and your bar opens out of it, its widgets appearing from left to right. Click anywhere or press Return to skip to its end, then **Get Started** (Return). Setup shows your wallpaper when StableBar can read it without a privacy prompt: one of Apple's, or a picture in your Pictures folder outside a Photos library. Otherwise it draws a soft backdrop in your Mac's appearance. Two steps follow, **Make it yours** and **Allow what your bar needs**, with your bar in the middle of the screen until it flies to the top edge of the main display, then **You're all set**. Setup covers the macOS menu bar until your bar takes its place, also while macOS asks for a permission. Every choice shows on your bar at once, and nothing is saved until **Done**. **Skip setup**, the **×** in the top-right corner or Esc, is there at every step. Reopen setup with **Getting Started…** in the menu-bar icon's menu, or **Run setup again** in Settings → Help; it plays the same intro each time.

Setup follows **System Settings → Accessibility → Display → Reduce motion** as you change it, without reopening setup. With it on, the intro only fades: your wallpaper, its softer copy, StableBar's name and your bar fade in where they rest, with no zoom, sweep or line; your bar fades from one place to the next instead of moving, and into the top edge instead of flying; each step fades out before the next fades in; and cards, tiles and rows change without moving.

### Make it yours

Your bar sits in the middle of the screen, over the top of your wallpaper, as it will look in the menu bar's place. Setup still covers the macOS menu bar, and keeps the same room for your bar whatever theme you choose, so the page doesn't move. Under it, five cards offer presets for its widgets, each with a symbol and a line saying what it's for. On a new configuration, **Everyday** is chosen when setup opens; on a bar you have changed, no preset is, so **Done** keeps your widgets unless you pick one. Click a card to put its widgets on your bar, and click it again to keep your bar as it was. Pointing at a card changes nothing. Tab and Shift-Tab reach the cards and the theme tiles even with macOS Keyboard navigation off; the focused one has a ring, and Space chooses it. VoiceOver reads each card's name, its widgets and whether it's chosen. On the top edge:

- **Everyday**: the Apple menu, the app's menus, Now playing in the center, Weather, Sound, Battery with its percentage, the date, the time and Menu bar items.
- **Minimal**: the Apple menu, the app's name, whose menus open when you point at it, Battery, the day, the time and Menu bar items.
- **Developer**: the Apple menu, Workspaces, the app's name with its menus on hover, Development servers, AI usage, CPU and memory with a meter beside each value, Battery, the date, the time and Menu bar items.
- **Workday**: the Apple menu, the app's menus, your next meeting in the center with a countdown and **Join**, Dock badges, Microphone & camera, Sound, Battery, the date, the time and Menu bar items.
- **Dashboard**: the Apple menu, Workspaces, the app's name with its menus on hover, Now playing in the center, Weather, rings for CPU, memory and battery, Sound, the date, the time and Menu bar items.

Below the macOS menu bar, which already shows the Apple menu, the app's menus, the date and time and its menu bar items, presets leave those out, and Minimal adds Workspaces, as numbers only, also when your bar already has Workspaces. On a Mac without a battery, such as a Mac mini, presets leave Battery out. The date and the time are two clocks, with the `secondary` and `time` roles so a theme can style each; they follow your Mac's date order and hour cycle. A preset chooses only widgets; the theme stays yours to choose.

On the starter bar, unchanged, a preset's bar takes its place: the starter's clock gives way to the preset's date and time, and a starter widget the preset doesn't have, such as Sound in Minimal, goes. On a bar you have changed, the preset's widgets replace yours too: a widget the preset doesn't have leaves your bar, and one from a package, such as a Store widget, or from an included file is turned off instead, so **Settings → Bar** can turn it on again. A widget of yours with the ID of one of the preset's stays as it is, in the preset's order; a turned-off one is turned on with its settings kept, and a Store widget moves to where the preset has it. A different widget with the same ID is kept, and a clock of yours with the ID `date` or `clock` keeps the preset's date and time out, so the date doesn't show twice. Active profile overrides still apply. Nothing is saved until **Done**, and **Skip setup** puts your bar back as it was. Choosing another preset, or clicking the chosen one again, changes only its widgets, Store widgets included: your theme, **Later** for Meetings and **Open at login** stay.

Under the presets, **Theme** has a tile for each of the [included themes](themes.md) in their order, starting with Clear, which a new bar has, then your installed ones. Each tile shows the top-right corner of your screen with your preset's bar in that theme, its pills included. Click a tile to put its theme on your bar; **Current** marks the theme you had, which is selected when setup opens. Seven tiles show at a time; with more themes, the row scrolls sideways. The bar's appearance and its clock follow macOS, and setup has no controls for them: **Settings → Theme** sets the appearance, and a clock's own settings its format.

**Found on your Mac**, the gold-ringed pill in the top-left corner, lists the Store packages the Store suggests for tools on your login shell's `PATH` or apps on your Mac, such as AI usage and Quick ask when `claude` or `codex` is there, and Homebrew outdated when `brew` is. A package already on your bar isn't listed, and the pill is hidden when the Store can't be reached. Nothing is downloaded until you click **Add**, which opens the same review; **Added** takes it off again. Choosing a preset keeps the packages you added. Skipping setup leaves them installed in **Settings → Store**, but not on your bar.

**Continue** (Return), in the toolbar at the bottom, goes on to Allow.

### Allow what your bar needs

One card lists what macOS must allow for the bar you chose, each row with its own button, and each row turns green as macOS answers. Your bar waits above it, a little smaller, until it can take its place.

**Take the menu bar's place** (Automation): **Allow…** lets StableBar control System Events, which turns on macOS's **Automatically hide and show the menu bar**; the first time, macOS asks, and the row shows that it's asking. Once it's allowed, or at once when the menu bar already hides, your bar flies to the top edge and takes the menu bar's place. If you don't allow it, or the change fails, your bar flies below the menu bar instead, which setup keeps covered: the row says so and offers **Open Settings…**, or **Allow…** to try again after a failure, and you can change it later in Settings. Allowing StableBar under **System Events** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation** while setup is open moves the bar to the top edge after all. Skipping setup puts the menu bar setting back. Quitting StableBar leaves the setting as it is, but StableBar remembers when setup or Settings turned auto-hide on, so [Uninstall](#uninstall) turns it off again; later, [**Automatically hide the menu bar**](#hide-the-native-menu-bar) in Settings turns it on or off.

A new configuration starts with the Apple menu, the frontmost app's menus, Sound, the clock and Menu bar items. When the first launch found the macOS menu bar showing, it put the bar below it with Workspaces, System and Sound instead; setup turns that unchanged starter into the one for the top edge, and back if the bar ends up below the menu bar. On a bar you have changed, including with `widgetOverrides`, `widgetOrder` or a screen's `layout`, in the file or in a profile, setup keeps your widgets; below the menu bar it removes only unchanged copies of the starter's Apple menu, app menus, clock and Menu bar items, and none when your configuration uses `includes` or packages, whose files can refer to them. Setup puts the bar on the top edge, or below the menu bar when macOS doesn't allow Automation, and resets a custom `bar.offset` to 0, so set it again afterwards if you use one. **Below the menu bar** and the bottom edge are in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**. A profile that sets `bar.offset`, `bar.belowMenuBar` or `bar.position` still applies over setup's choice.

**Open menus from the bar** (Accessibility, or **Device Control and Data Access** from macOS 27): **Allow…** opens **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility** with a panel at the bottom of its window. Drag the StableBar tile from the panel into the list and confirm with Touch ID or your password; if StableBar is already in the list, turn its switch on instead. The panel closes once access is allowed, when you quit System Settings, or with its close button. The app menus, the Apple menu, Menu bar items, Workspaces on macOS Spaces and window clearance depend on this access, so **Finish setup** waits until macOS allows it; setup checks every second, and **Allow…** opens the pane again. Until access is allowed, a warning icon takes the app's menus' place, and clicking the Apple logo asks for access. While setup is open, App menus leave your bar instead, and your bar on the stage shows the app that was in front when setup opened, with sample menus such as File, Edit and View. Setup never asks for Screen Recording: a [menu bar item shown as in the menu bar](widgets.md#menu-bar-items) asks for it when you choose **As in the menu bar**, and **Settings → General → Access** lists it while your bar has one. Nor does it ask for Location: a [`wifi` provider](providers.md) asks for it the first time it updates, to show the network's name, and **Settings → General → Access** lists it while your bar has one.

**See your next meeting** (Calendars), while your bar has Meetings or another calendar widget: **Allow…** asks macOS; once it has asked, **Open Settings…** opens **Privacy & Security → Calendars**. **Later** saves Meetings turned off, so you can finish without Calendars, and **Undo** takes that back; turn Meetings on later in **Settings → Bar**. Existing calendar widgets stay unchanged. Picking a preset with Meetings doesn't open the Calendars dialog, and a newly chosen Meetings stays off your bar until you allow Calendars.

**Switch desktops from the bar**, optional, when Workspaces uses macOS Spaces. **Open Keyboard Shortcuts…** opens System Settings: choose **Mission Control** and turn on **Switch to Desktop N** for each desktop you need. The row turns green when all are on; its tooltip says how many desktops can't be switched from the bar yet and names desktops beyond macOS's shortcut range. It never holds up setup: you can turn the shortcuts on later in **Settings → General → Access**, and it checks again when you return to StableBar.

Under them, the preset's [Store](packages.md) widgets, each with what it does: Now playing, Weather, Development servers, AI usage and Dock badges run commands. Each one's **Add** opens the same review as **Add here** in **Edit Bar in Place**, showing what it runs: **Add to bar** installs it and puts it where the preset has it, and **Back** leaves it off your bar, still with **Add**. A Store widget already on your bar, or one you added under **Found on your Mac**, shows as added. AI usage comes only when `claude` or `codex` is on your login shell's `PATH`, and starts in its one-line layout. When the Store can't be reached, presets add only their built-in widgets, and the card names the Store widgets the preset leaves out.

**Finish setup** (Return) waits until macOS has answered for Automation, Accessibility is allowed, and Calendars is allowed or set for later. **Back** returns to Make it yours, and your bar flies back to the middle of the screen.

**System** shows CPU and memory use with details in its popup; **Meetings** shows your next meeting, with a countdown and **Join** before it starts and the time left while it runs, and today's agenda in its popup; **Battery** shows the charge with details in its popup; **Sound** shows the volume; [**Menu bar items**](widgets.md#menu-bar-items) lists the items in the macOS menu bar. These widgets use native providers and run no scripts.

**Workspaces** uses AeroSpace, yabai, OmniWM or Rift when one is installed and running: AeroSpace and yabai on your login shell's `PATH`, OmniWM as its app or `omniwmctl` on the `PATH`, Rift as `rift-cli` on the `PATH`. When setup picks the source, or you add a Workspaces widget, StableBar keeps the source your configuration names while that one is installed, and otherwise takes the first one installed and running, in that order; a running bar keeps its source and doesn't switch on its own. When the first launch finds none, for example because your login shell's `PATH` arrives late, its starter uses macOS Spaces. Setup looks for a manager once, when it opens: until a setup finishes, reopening setup looks again, as long as that starter is unchanged. yabai needs [its signals](widgets.md#yabai-workspaces), OmniWM needs [**Enable IPC**](widgets.md#omniwm-workspaces), and Rift needs [each Space turned on](widgets.md#rift-workspaces); **Settings → General → Workspaces** shows whether each one is connected. Without any of them, Workspaces shows your [macOS Spaces](widgets.md#macos-spaces-workspaces), which switch desktops through the **Switch to Desktop** shortcuts and need Accessibility access to do it.

Setup turns on [**Keep windows clear of StableBar**](#keeping-windows-clear-of-the-bar) when it detects no window manager running, and leaves it off when it detects one; its switch is in **Settings → General**. An explicit `bar.keepWindowsClear` value in your configuration, including an included file or an active profile, keeps its value. Window clearance keeps following your bar as it was when setup opened, including earlier unsaved command-line changes, so no window moves for a choice you might skip; it follows setup's choices once you finish. Setup leaves **Keep notifications clear** as it is. With AeroSpace, yabai or OmniWM, **Settings → General → Windows and notifications** gives the gap for your bar.

### You're all set

After **Finish setup**, the backdrop fades, a warm line shows under your bar, now in its place, and the last card drops in, with StableBar's icon and a check. **Open at login** starts StableBar when you log in, and starts on in an Applications folder: in the menu bar's place, macOS keeps its menu bar hidden even while StableBar isn't running. Turn it off to leave it off. **Done** (Return) saves everything; **Edit Bar in Place** saves and then turns the bar into the editor. If macOS requires Login Items approval, the card says so with **Allow StableBar in Login Items…**, and **Done** then closes it. If Open at login can't be changed, the card says so with your bar already saved, and **Done** then closes it. If a permission was taken back since **Finish setup**, **Done** returns to Allow. When Meetings is set for later, the card reminds you that it stays off.

Outside Applications, the card offers **Move to Applications** instead of the switch. It moves StableBar and relaunches it at this card, with your choices and configuration file kept. It uses `/Applications` when writable, otherwise `~/Applications`, without asking for an administrator password. It copies the app into Applications, then moves the original to the Trash once it confirms the new copy is running. An original on a read-only disk image or running with App Translocation stays where it is. An older copy in Applications needs your confirmation and goes to the Trash only once the new copy opens; the same or a newer version offers **Use the One in Applications** instead, which opens that copy and leaves the original where it is. Quit a destination copy that is already running before trying again. If moving fails, the row explains why and offers **Show in Finder**. After a successful relaunch, Open at login is on; registration still waits for **Done**. If macOS reports that the new copy could not open and it is not running, setup returns to the original with Open at login left off. If saving setup's choices fails, StableBar still reopens the original and explains how to continue. If launching times out or cannot be confirmed, both copies stay in place; a note explains how to open StableBar from Applications with Finder. If the Applications copy has changed or opened during recovery, every copy stays in place and the note lists their locations. A previous copy kept for manual recovery is named `StableBar (previous).app` beside the Applications copy, or `StableBar (previous 2).app` and so on if that name is taken. If it cannot be renamed, the note names its hidden location: press ⇧⌘. (Shift-Command-Period) in Finder to show it. These recovery copies stay until you choose what to keep. StableBar only tries to move leftover previous copies from completed moves to the Trash on its next launch from Applications. While setup is open, Settings directs you back to setup's last card to move the app. Setup can resume for 15 minutes after the move.

Until **Done**, your choices are temporary changes, like command-line changes without `--persist`: the bar shows them, but the file is unchanged. Setup saves your choices and keeps your scripts and imported files; your widgets stay unless you pick a preset, whose widgets replace them. **Skip setup** (Esc) puts your bar back as it was; once you have changed something, it first asks **Skip setup?**, where **Keep Setting Up** (Return or Esc) takes you back. Permission requests act immediately through macOS. Setup opens by itself at launch until you finish or skip it. After that, **Getting Started…** in the menu-bar icon's menu and **Run setup again** in Settings → Help open it. While a skipped setup was never finished, **Settings → General → Access** also shows **Finish setting up**.

Widgets are the items on your bar. Packages provide extra widgets or themes. After setup, **Edit Bar in Place** turns the bar itself into the editor; **Settings → Bar** changes content, appearance and behavior. The menu-bar icon also links to this user guide.

## Download a release

Stable releases after v0.4.0 on the [releases page](https://github.com/stablebox/StableBar/releases) include `StableBar.dmg`. Betas of the next version are marked **Pre-release**. Both are built for Apple silicon. Releases signed with a Developer ID are notarized and open like any app. Earlier ones are ad-hoc signed, so macOS blocks them the first time you open them: allow it in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway**.

To install the latest stable release with [Homebrew](https://brew.sh) instead, run `brew install --cask stablebox/tap/stablebar`.

## First launch

The first launch creates `~/.config/stablebar/config.json` from [Resources/config.json](../Resources/config.json), with the bar on the top edge, or below the menu bar when the menu bar doesn't hide, where it starts with Workspaces, System and Sound. Subsequent launches keep your file. If it is a symbolic link, Setup, Settings and command-line saves update the linked file and keep the link. Right-click the bar or use its macOS menu-bar icon to choose **Edit Bar in Place**, **Try Themes…**, **Get Widgets…**, [**Share My Bar…**](#share-your-bar), **Settings…** or **Quit StableBar**; the menu-bar icon also offers [**Hide Bar**](#hide-the-bar). **Get Widgets…** opens **Settings → Store**. While editing, the menus offer **Finish Editing Bar**. **Settings → General** controls login, window and notification clearance, access and workspace connections, opens or reloads the configuration file, and links to updates, setup and help. Placement is in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**.

Keep personal settings in `~/.config/stablebar/config.json` and personal scripts in `~/.config/stablebar/widgets/`, outside the repository. Point commands directly at those scripts. `Resources/config.json` is the shared starter configuration; rebuilding the app does not overwrite your personal file. Local scripts should load any supporting files from their own directory.

StableBar sits at the top edge of each display, or, with `bar.belowMenuBar` (**Below the menu bar** in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**), right under each display's macOS menu bar; a display whose menu bar hides keeps the bar on its top edge. `bar.offset` moves the bar further down from there. It hides on native fullscreen Spaces, and when you [hide it](#hide-the-bar).

With `"position": "bottom"` (**Edge → Bottom** in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**), the bar sits on the bottom edge of each display instead, and `bar.offset` moves it up from that edge. It counts from the display's edge, not from the Dock: with the Dock at the bottom and always shown, the Dock covers the bar, so hide the Dock, move it to a side, or raise the bar with `bar.offset`. At the bottom, popups and tooltips open upward, `belowBar` underlines sit on the bar's top side, the notch positions place widgets as on a display without a notch, and `belowMenuBar` has no effect. A screen setup or kind of screen can set its own `position` in its `bar`, like `offset`. AeroSpace gaps are managed separately. Of the macOS menu-bar settings, StableBar changes only auto-hide, in setup and in [Settings](#hide-the-native-menu-bar). Keep the center clear on a notched display. The default configuration uses only the left and right groups.

## Hide the bar

Choose **Hide Bar** in the menu-bar icon's menu, or run `StableBar --hide`, to put the bar away on every display, for example while you present or watch a video. **Show Bar** or `StableBar --show` brings it back, and `StableBar --toggle` switches between the two; see [Command-line controls](command-line.md). The bar stays hidden until you show it or quit StableBar, and your configuration file doesn't change.

To hide it while an app is in front, set `bar.hidden` in a [profile](configuration.md#theme-tokens-and-automatic-profiles):

```json
{ "name": "tv", "when": { "app": "com.apple.TV" }, "configuration": { "bar": { "hidden": true } } }
```

The bar shows only when neither **Hide Bar** nor the configuration hides it, so **Show Bar** doesn't bring back a bar that `bar.hidden` hides. While the bar is hidden, its popup closes, providers set to `whenVisible` pause as they do when none of their widgets is visible, and [window and notification clearance](#keeping-windows-clear-of-the-bar) leaves windows where they are.

## Share your bar

Choose **Share My Bar…** in the bar's right-click menu or the menu-bar icon's menu, or the button above the bar's preview in **Settings → Bar**, to share your bar. The right-click menu uses that screen's bar; the menu-bar icon uses the screen under the pointer, and Settings the screen it shows. As with Settings, StableBar appears in the Dock while the panel is open, and clicking its Dock icon brings the panel back when Settings isn't open. The panel has no title bar and floats above other windows: drag its background to move it, and close it with **×** at the top right, Esc or ⌘W. A larger panel shows a larger preview, over a blur of the image. It makes an image of your bar for social media: the bar as it is now, with its real data and the time, at its place over your desktop wallpaper. It needs no screen-recording permission.

Pick a screen in the menu at the top left:

- **This screen**: the bar as it is on the screen you shared from, at that screen's width. On a screen with a notch, **Notch** in the same menu draws it and lays the bar out around it; turn it off to leave the notch out.
- **13″ MacBook Air** (1470 points wide), **14″ MacBook Pro** (1512) or **16″ MacBook Pro** (1728): the bar laid out for that MacBook's screen at its default size, with your laptop screen's layout and look. **Notch** draws the notch and lays the bar out around it; turn it off for a MacBook without one. A narrower screen gives a narrower image, where the bar shows larger.

And a shape in the toolbar under the image:

- **Bar**: the bar across the screen's width, on a strip of wallpaper, at twice the screen's width in pixels.
- **16:9** (3200 × 1800), **1:1** (2160 × 2160) or **4:5** (2160 × 2700): the top of your desktop, or its bottom with a bar at the bottom, scaled to the image's width. When the image is taller than the screen, the wallpaper fills it.

The image carries StableBar's badge, its logo, name and website, in the bottom-right corner, or the top-right one with a bar at the bottom, so it sits on the wallpaper beyond the bar. To leave it out, point at the image and click **×** on the badge, or turn off **StableBar Badge** in the menu at the top left. **+ Badge** on the image, or the same menu, brings it back. StableBar remembers your choice for the next image.

The toolbar's buttons copy the image to the clipboard as PNG, save it to a file, open the bar link, and **Share** (or Return) opens the macOS share menu for AirDrop, Messages, Mail and more. A dynamic or aerial wallpaper that has no still image is replaced with StableBar's own backdrop.

The link button opens your [bar link](themes.md) above the toolbar: anyone with StableBar can try your look and your built-in and Store widgets on their own bar, and keep the widgets they like. **Copy Link** copies it. **What it shares** opens a list of your look and each widget the link carries, with the settings a Store widget takes along, and under **Stays on your Mac**, the widgets it leaves out and why, such as a script widget, a custom provider, or a package from a folder or a private source, and the Store settings it doesn't carry: secrets and free text. Click the link button again, or press Esc, to close it.

## Updates

A release signed with a Developer ID checks GitHub for a newer version shortly after launch and every four minutes. These silent checks pause while an update check, download or installation is already active, and stop making requests when automatic checks are off. They keep running while an update waits to be downloaded, so a newer release replaces it in the popup, and the tile goes away when no update is left, for example after switching back to the Stable track. A failed check retries at the next interval. Sparkle still chooses the compatible release and handles its download and installation. As soon as a check finds one, a filled circular download icon with a small theme-accent dot fades in at the right end of the bar and moves the neighboring widgets aside. Reduce Motion disables that animation. Click it to see the changes from every release published after your installed version through the offered version, grouped newest first, each with its author's GitHub name when the release notes give one. The popup loads this history from GitHub, including releases no longer in the update feed. Its height stays at 420 points, with scrolling release notes and the download action kept below them. If the history cannot be loaded, it shows the offered release's notes and a link to all changes on GitHub. **Download** checks again and downloads the newest compatible release on your update track, so an older pending offer does not require an intermediate installation. Then choose **Install and Relaunch** once it's downloaded. Updates already downloaded keep their install action. The bar button stays icon-only and changes to a filled circular sync icon when the update is ready; its tooltip describes the current stage. Download and preparation progress appear inside the popup without increasing its height. The popup also shows **Enjoying StableBar? Star it on GitHub** above the download button until you dismiss it with ×. When the [GitHub CLI](https://cli.github.com) `gh` is on your login shell's `PATH` and signed in, clicking it stars StableBar on github.com as that account, which its tooltip names, and says thanks; the line doesn't come back, and it doesn't appear at all once `gh` sees that account has starred StableBar. Without `gh`, or when the star fails, clicking it opens the GitHub repository, where you can star StableBar yourself, and the line stays until you dismiss it. **Star on GitHub** in Settings → Help works the same way, even after you dismiss the line, and reads **Starred on GitHub** once `gh` sees the star. **Check for Updates…** in the menu-bar icon's menu checks right away, also while an update waits to be downloaded, and so does **Check now** in **Settings → General → Updates** when no update is waiting. **Settings → General → Updates** also turns off automatic checks, or lets StableBar download updates and install them when it quits.

**Update track** chooses the releases you get. **Stable** gets stable releases. **Beta** gets betas of the next version and stable releases, whichever is newer. An installed beta starts on Beta. Switching back to Stable keeps your beta until a newer stable release is out, because StableBar never installs an older version.

Beta version names include a UTC timestamp and build number, such as `0.5.0-beta.20261020103000.114`, to keep new betas in order on GitHub. In-app updates compare the build number.

Only an app signed with a Developer ID updates itself, because the update must be signed by the same developer as the app it replaces. Ad-hoc signed releases and the builds you make yourself don't check for updates; **Settings → General → Updates** links to the releases page instead.

## Delay the native menu bar

When the macOS menu bar hides automatically, **Settings → General → Native menu bar → Reveal delay** sets how long to hold the pointer at the top edge before it appears. Enter milliseconds: `1000` is one second, `2000` is two, and `3000` is three. `0`, the default, adds no delay. Allow StableBar in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility** (**Device Control and Data Access** from macOS 27).

StableBar holds mouse movement six points below each display's top edge until the delay expires, out of reach of the band where macOS 27 reveals the menu bar by itself. Moving away cancels the wait. Drag events pass through. This works while menu-bar auto-hide is enabled and StableBar is running; turning the delay off or quitting restores normal pointer behavior. The wait is added before macOS's own reveal animation.

Set `bar.menuBarRevealDelay` in JSON, including in an included file or a profile. See the [menu-bar delay example](../examples/menu-bar-delay/config.json):

```json
{ "bar": { "menuBarRevealDelay": 2000 } }
```

## Hide the native menu bar

**Settings → General → Native menu bar → Automatically hide the menu bar** turns macOS's **Automatically hide and show the menu bar** on or off, as setup's **Allow** turns it on. Turn it on when the bar sits on the top edge in the menu bar's place, and off to show the menu bar again, for example after turning on **Below the menu bar** in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**. It changes the macOS setting through System Events, so it stays after StableBar quits. The first time, macOS asks to let StableBar control System Events; without that access, the toggle stays as it is and **Open Settings…** opens **Privacy & Security → Automation**, where you turn on **System Events** under StableBar. Back in StableBar, the change applies. **System Settings → Menu Bar**, or **Control Center** before macOS 26, shows the same setting. [Uninstall](#uninstall) turns it off when StableBar turned it on.

On macOS 27, while the menu bar hides automatically, a display with a notch shows the menu bar when you click where it would be. StableBar keeps its bar above that area there, so clicks reach the bar, and steps under the menu bar while macOS shows it.

**Settings → General → Native menu bar → Always hide** keeps the macOS menu bar invisible while StableBar runs, even when the pointer reaches the top edge. Quitting StableBar shows it again. In JSON, set `"bar": { "hideMenuBar": true }`.

Without it, the menu bar shows on hover as usual. StableBar makes it invisible only while its [Control Center and Menu bar items](widgets.md#menu-bar-items) widgets open an item, and shows it again once the item closes.

## Keeping windows clear of the bar

macOS provides no public API for a custom bar to reserve screen space. Without a tiling window manager, enable **Settings → General → Windows and notifications → Keep windows clear of the bar** (`bar.keepWindowsClear`, off by default; setup turns it on when no window manager runs) and allow StableBar in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility** (**Device Control and Data Access** from macOS 27). StableBar then moves overlapping ordinary windows below the bar, or above a bar at the bottom, and reduces their height when needed to keep them clear of the Dock and the menu bar. It follows each display's bar height, offset and scale, and waits until you finish dragging before adjusting a window, including when you drag it back to a position StableBar already corrected. See [the window clearance example](../examples/window-clearance/config.json).

Native fullscreen windows, minimized windows and dialogs are left alone. Some apps do not expose movable or resizable windows, or enforce a minimum size that cannot fit; StableBar cannot keep those clear. Disabling the option stops adjustments and leaves windows where they are. Revoking Accessibility access pauses adjustments; granting it resumes them. A locally rebuilt, ad-hoc signed app may need to be authorized again.

Leave this option off when another window manager controls your gaps. With AeroSpace, set `gaps.outer.top` in `~/.aerospace.toml` to the bar's panel height (40 by default), plus any `bar.offset` beyond the menu bar; AeroSpace accepts per-monitor values. yabai users can set `external_bar` with the same clearance. OmniWM measures `gaps.outer.top` in its `settings.toml` from the display's top edge, menu bar included, so set it to the panel height plus `bar.offset`, plus the menu bar's height when the bar sits below a menu bar that shows. With Rift, set `top` under `[settings.layout.gaps.outer]` in `~/.config/rift/config.toml`. For a bar at the bottom, set the bottom gap instead, such as `gaps.outer.bottom` in AeroSpace, to the panel height plus `bar.offset`. A below-bar underline adds its maximum configured gap plus thickness beyond the existing bottom margin. For example, a 38 pt bar with no margin, a 2 pt underline and a 3 pt gap needs 43 pt. With display scale `s`, round upward to whole screen points: `max(ceil(height × s), ceil(height × s − margin + maximum gap × s) + ceil(maximum thickness × s))`. StableBar reserves this maximum across states, so focus or mode changes never move windows or resize panels.

When AeroSpace, yabai or OmniWM runs and one of its windows starts under the bar, **Settings → General → Windows and notifications** warns about it and give the setting for your bar, such as `outer.top = 40` under `[gaps]` in `~/.aerospace.toml`. For AeroSpace it names the file AeroSpace reads, `~/.aerospace.toml` or `aerospace/aerospace.toml` in `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` (`~/.config` by default). When the window manager's configuration file exists, a button such as **Open aerospace.toml** opens it in your app for plain text. StableBar reads the windows' frames through Accessibility when you open that section, and again when you click **Check Again**. It never changes the window manager's configuration.

On macOS 26 or later, enable **Settings → General → Windows and notifications → Keep notifications clear of the bar** (`bar.keepNotificationsClear`, off by default) to move notification banners 16 points below the bar on the display that shows them. It uses the same Accessibility access and can stay on with AeroSpace, yabai or OmniWM. The Notification Center panel and desktop widgets stay where macOS puts them. This depends on Notification Center's Accessibility structure and does not support macOS 14 or 15: Settings shows the switch disabled with **Needs macOS 26 or later.** A configuration shared with a newer Mac can still set `bar.keepNotificationsClear`; older macOS versions keep the saved value without moving notifications.

## Uninstall

**Settings → General → Uninstall → Uninstall…** removes StableBar and undoes what it changed on your Mac; search Settings for "uninstall" to find it. Its confirmation lists each step, then StableBar:

- quits any other StableBar that's running. If one doesn't quit, Uninstall stops before changing anything;
- turns off the macOS menu bar's auto-hide when setup or Settings turned it on. Auto-hide that StableBar didn't turn on, for example because you hid the menu bar before installing it, stays on unless you check **Turn off the macOS menu bar's auto-hide**. Setups finished before Uninstall existed didn't record this, so check it if setup hid your menu bar. Auto-hide that setup turned off stays off;
- turns off **Open at login**;
- removes the `stablebar` command that [**Command line**](command-line.md#install-the-command) installed, asking for an administrator password when its folder needs one. A `stablebar` command that StableBar didn't write stays;
- deletes package and source tokens from the Keychain;
- quits. Once it has quit, it resets its Accessibility, Screen Recording, Calendars and Automation permissions, deletes its caches and preferences and moves StableBar to the Trash. These steps wait so the bar doesn't keep running without its permissions or its app.

**Also move ~/.config/stablebar to the Trash** is off by default, because that folder holds your configuration, scripts and packages. A configuration elsewhere, opened with `--config`, stays. If a step fails, for example because you cancel the password prompt, the other steps still run. StableBar lists what's left to do by hand and stays whole until you click **Quit StableBar**, which finishes the rest, unless another StableBar kept running: then quit that one and uninstall again. If StableBar can't start the steps that wait for it to quit, it lists them too, to do by hand. If a step fails after StableBar has quit, an alert lists what's left to do by hand.

When Homebrew installed StableBar, the app stays in Applications: run **Uninstall** first and click **Quit StableBar**, then run `brew uninstall --cask stablebar`. `brew uninstall --zap --cask stablebar` does both: it runs `stablebar --uninstall --yes`, then also deletes `~/.config/stablebar`.

`stablebar --uninstall` does the same from Terminal; see [Command-line controls](command-line.md).

If StableBar is already gone, undo its changes by hand:

1. Turn off **Automatically hide and show the menu bar** in **System Settings → Menu Bar**, or **Control Center** before macOS 26.
2. Remove StableBar from **System Settings → General → Login Items** and from **Accessibility** (**Device Control and Data Access** from macOS 27), **Screen Recording**, **Calendars**, **Automation** and **Location Services** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
3. Run `sudo rm /usr/local/bin/stablebar` if StableBar installed that command, and `security delete-generic-password -s StableBar` until it finds no item.
4. Delete `~/.config/stablebar`, `~/Library/Caches/org.stablebar.app`, `~/Library/Caches/org.stablebar.app.sparkle`, `~/Library/Caches/StableBar`, `~/Library/HTTPStorages/org.stablebar.app` and `~/Library/Preferences/org.stablebar.app.plist`.
