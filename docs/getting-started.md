# Getting started

## Install and set up

Open `StableBar.dmg`, drag StableBar to Applications, then open the app. Setup starts with a tour: StableBar's badge appears over slowly shifting amber colors, and **Get Started** (Return) plays a short demo. It builds a bar from widgets, clicks two of them open and tries a few themes, then flies up into your own bar; **Skip intro** (Esc) jumps to that last part. When the macOS menu bar is visible, the demo bar first stops and asks **Make it your menu bar.**: **Replace the menu bar** (Return) hides the macOS menu bar and lands the bar on the top edge in its place, and **Keep the menu bar** (Esc) lands it below. Five steps then let you pick a preset, a theme and appearance, and a placement and clock, choose access, window clearance and Open at login, and try the bar itself. Each choice shows on your real bar right away; in the last step, click a widget to open its popup. **Finish setup** then saves everything. Reopen it with **Getting Started…** in the menu-bar icon's menu, or **Settings → General → Help → Open setup…**. With Reduce Motion on, the tour skips its opening animation.

Widgets are the items on your bar. Packages provide extra widgets or themes. After setup, **Edit Bar in Place** turns the bar itself into the editor; **Open Settings** opens **Settings → Bar**, where you can change content, appearance and behavior. The menu-bar icon also links to this user guide.

The first step offers presets: **Minimal** keeps the active app and the time on Built-in; **Developer** adds Workspaces and System on Blueprint in Dark; **Workday** adds Meetings on Léman; **Notch** puts the Notch theme on the top edge; **Everything** adds Workspaces, System and Meetings on Rosé Pine. A preset fills in the style step, where you can change any choice, and chooses the [built-in widgets](widgets.md) to add. Click the chosen preset again to keep your current bar.

Setup shows your current theme, appearance, placement and clock. **Keep current** leaves a setting untouched. Choose from the [themes](themes.md) that ship with StableBar or those you have installed; **Light**, **Dark** and **Automatic** control the bar's appearance. The bar shows the effect of active profiles, and the last step names any profile that still controls a setting, with a button to edit it. Setup saves your defaults and preserves custom widgets, scripts and imported files.

A new configuration places the bar on the top edge when the macOS menu bar hides automatically, and below the menu bar otherwise; existing configurations retain their position. Choosing **Top edge** in setup, or the Notch preset, turns on **Automatically hide and show the menu bar**, so the menu bar no longer covers the bar; **Below menu bar** turns it off. Setup makes this change through System Events, so the first time macOS asks to let StableBar control System Events. Skipping or closing setup puts the menu bar setting back. Neither placement reserves space for windows. Setup's fourth step asks for Accessibility access, offers **Keep windows clear of StableBar** and **Keep notifications clear of StableBar**, and gives separate guidance when it detects AeroSpace or yabai. See [Keeping windows clear of the bar](#keeping-windows-clear-of-the-bar).

**System** shows CPU and memory use with details in its popup; **Meetings** shows your next meeting and today's agenda; **Workspaces** is added only when AeroSpace or yabai is on your login shell's `PATH`. Workspaces prefers your configured source and uses the other one when only that one is installed; yabai still needs [its signals](widgets.md#yabai-workspaces). These widgets use native providers and run no scripts. A preset adds widgets but never removes any. A matching widget already on your bar stays as it is; a turned-off one is turned on with its settings kept. A different widget with the same ID is kept. Active profile overrides still apply.

The starter bar needs no special access. Setup's fourth step lists Accessibility, which clearance and menu widgets use, and Calendars when a calendar widget needs it, shows their status and offers a permission action or System Settings link. Status updates when you return from System Settings. A newly chosen Meetings widget stays off your bar during the tour until you allow Calendar access, so macOS does not prompt in the middle of it. Allow Calendar access in the fourth step, or choose **Set up Meetings later**: finishing without access saves that widget turned off, so it does not prompt immediately after setup. Enable it later in **Bar**. Existing calendar widgets stay unchanged. Scripts that control other apps ask for Automation access when used; AeroSpace and yabai manage their own Accessibility permission.

Until **Finish setup**, your choices are temporary changes, like command-line changes without `--persist`: the bar shows them, but the file is unchanged. **Skip setup** (Esc) puts your bar back as it was. Permission buttons act immediately through macOS. Open at login is off until enabled and requires the app to be in Applications. If macOS requires Login Items approval, setup shows the remaining action. A setup that has never been completed opens again on the next launch; you can always reopen it from **Settings → General**.

## Download a release

Stable releases after v0.4.0 on the [releases page](https://github.com/Seb-GRAF/StableBar/releases) include `StableBar.dmg`. Betas of the next version are marked **Pre-release**. Both are built for Apple silicon. Releases signed with a Developer ID are notarized and open like any app. Earlier ones are ad-hoc signed, so macOS blocks them the first time you open them: allow it in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway**.

## First launch

The first launch creates `~/.config/stablebar/config.json` from [Resources/config.json](../Resources/config.json), with the bar on the top edge, or below the menu bar when the menu bar doesn't hide. Subsequent launches keep your file. If it is a symbolic link, Setup, Settings and command-line saves update the linked file and keep the link. Right-click the bar or use its macOS menu-bar icon to choose **Edit Bar in Place**, **Try Themes…**, **Settings…** or **Quit StableBar**. While editing, the menus offer **Finish Editing Bar**. **Settings → General** controls login, window and notification clearance, access and workspace connections, opens or reloads the configuration file, and links to updates, setup and help. Placement is in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**.

Keep personal settings in `~/.config/stablebar/config.json` and personal scripts in `~/.config/stablebar/widgets/`, outside the repository. Point commands directly at those scripts. `Resources/config.json` is the shared starter configuration; rebuilding the app does not overwrite your personal file. Local scripts should load any supporting files from their own directory.

StableBar sits at the top edge of each display. `bar.offset` moves it down from that edge. It hides on native fullscreen Spaces. AeroSpace gaps and macOS menu-bar settings are managed separately; this app does not change them. Keep the center clear on a notched display. The default configuration uses only the left and right groups.

## Updates

A release signed with a Developer ID checks GitHub for a newer version shortly after launch and every four minutes. These silent checks pause while an update check, offer, download or installation is already active, and stop making requests when automatic checks are off. A failed check retries at the next interval. Sparkle still chooses the compatible release and handles its download and installation. As soon as a check finds one, a filled circular download icon with a small theme-accent dot fades in at the right end of the bar and moves the neighboring widgets aside. Reduce Motion disables that animation. Click it to see the changes from every release published after your installed version through the offered version, grouped newest first. The popup loads this history from GitHub, including releases no longer in the update feed. Its height stays at 420 points, with scrolling release notes and the download action kept below them. If the history cannot be loaded, it shows the offered release's notes and a link to all changes on GitHub. **Download** checks again and downloads the newest compatible release on your update track, so an older pending offer does not require an intermediate installation. Then choose **Install and Relaunch** once it's downloaded. Updates already downloaded keep their install action. The bar button stays icon-only and changes to a filled circular sync icon when the update is ready; its tooltip describes the current stage. Download and preparation progress appear inside the popup without increasing its height. **Check for Updates…** in the menu-bar icon's menu or in **Settings → General → Updates** checks right away and remains available while an update waits. **Settings → General → Updates** also turns off automatic checks, or lets StableBar download updates and install them when it quits.

**Update track** chooses the releases you get. **Stable** gets stable releases. **Beta** gets betas of the next version and stable releases, whichever is newer. An installed beta starts on Beta. Switching back to Stable keeps your beta until a newer stable release is out, because StableBar never installs an older version.

Beta version names include a UTC timestamp and build number, such as `0.5.0-beta.20261020103000.114`, to keep new betas in order on GitHub. In-app updates compare the build number.

Only an app signed with a Developer ID updates itself, because the update must be signed by the same developer as the app it replaces. Ad-hoc signed releases and the builds you make yourself don't check for updates; **Settings → General → Updates** links to the releases page instead.

## Keeping windows clear of the bar

macOS provides no public API for a custom bar to reserve screen space. Without a tiling window manager, enable **Settings → General → Windows and notifications → Keep windows clear of the bar** (`bar.keepWindowsClear`, off by default) and allow StableBar in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility**. StableBar then moves overlapping ordinary windows below the bar and reduces their height when needed to keep them above the Dock. It follows each display's bar height, offset and scale, and waits until you finish dragging before adjusting a window. See [the window clearance example](../examples/window-clearance/config.json).

Native fullscreen windows, minimized windows and dialogs are left alone. Some apps do not expose movable or resizable windows, or enforce a minimum size that cannot fit; StableBar cannot keep those clear. Disabling the option stops adjustments and leaves windows where they are. Revoking Accessibility access pauses adjustments; granting it resumes them. A locally rebuilt, ad-hoc signed app may need to be authorized again.

Leave this option off when another window manager controls your gaps. With AeroSpace, set `gaps.outer.top` in `~/.aerospace.toml` to the bar's panel height (40 by default), plus any `bar.offset` beyond the menu bar; AeroSpace accepts per-monitor values. yabai users can set `external_bar` with the same clearance. A below-bar underline adds its maximum configured gap plus thickness beyond the existing bottom margin. For example, a 38 pt bar with no margin, a 2 pt underline and a 3 pt gap needs 43 pt. With display scale `s`, round upward to whole screen points: `max(ceil(height × s), ceil(height × s − margin + maximum gap × s) + ceil(maximum thickness × s))`. StableBar reserves this maximum across states, so focus or mode changes never move windows or resize panels.

On macOS 26, enable **Settings → General → Windows and notifications → Keep notifications clear of the bar** (`bar.keepNotificationsClear`, off by default) to move notification banners 16 points below the bar on the display that shows them. It uses the same Accessibility access and can stay on with AeroSpace or yabai. The Notification Center panel and desktop widgets stay where macOS puts them. This depends on Notification Center's Accessibility structure and does not support macOS 14 or 15.
