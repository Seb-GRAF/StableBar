# Getting started

## Install and set up

Open `StableBar.dmg`, drag StableBar to Applications, then open the app. Setup starts with a tour: a felt-pen line swells into a glass bar under StableBar's name, drawn like the [website](https://stablebar.app/) over colors that shift slowly from day to dusk, and **Get Started** (Return) plays a short demo in that bar. It builds a bar from widgets, clicks two of them open and tries a few themes. The last theme turns it into your own bar, which flies up into place; **Skip intro** (Esc) jumps to that last part. When the macOS menu bar is visible, the demo bar first stops and asks **Make it your menu bar.**: **Replace the menu bar** (Return) hides the macOS menu bar and lands the bar on the top edge in its place, and **Keep the menu bar** (Esc) lands it below. Five steps then ask where the bar goes, let you pick a preset, a theme, an appearance and the clock, choose window clearance and Open at login, ask for the permissions that bar needs, and let you try the bar itself; when the intro asked where the bar goes, setup starts at the second step, and **Back** returns to the first. Your bar previews your choices right away; window and notification clearance changes wait for **Finish setup**. In the last step, click a widget to open its popup. **Finish setup** then saves everything. Reopen it with **Getting Started…** in the menu-bar icon's menu, or **Settings → General → Help → Open setup…**. The intro plays only the first time, before you finish or skip setup, and not with Reduce Motion on: then setup starts at its first step, where **Watch the intro** plays it.

Setup follows **System Settings → Accessibility → Display → Reduce motion** as you change it, without reopening setup. With it on, each step fades out before the next fades in, without blur or movement. The selected theme comes into view immediately, and cards and buttons keep their highlights without growing or shrinking. Progress dots and permission rows update without movement, and the completion window opens and closes without scaling.

Widgets are the items on your bar. Packages provide extra widgets or themes. After setup, **Edit Bar in Place** turns the bar itself into the editor; **Open Settings** opens **Settings → Bar**, where you can change content, appearance and behavior. The menu-bar icon also links to this user guide.

The first step asks **Make it your menu bar.**, like the intro. **Replace the menu bar**, the larger choice and the one setup recommends, puts StableBar on the top edge and hides the macOS menu bar; **Keep the menu bar**, under it, puts StableBar below the menu bar, which stays visible. Neither is picked until you choose one, whatever the bar's placement, and **Continue** waits until the macOS menu bar hides or shows to match your choice; answering the intro's question counts. Either choice also resets a custom `bar.offset` to 0, so set it again afterwards if you use one. Hiding or showing the menu bar needs Automation, which macOS asks for right there, and **Continue** waits until you allow it. **Replace the menu bar** while it already hides, or **Keep the menu bar** while it shows, needs no Automation. When an active profile sets `bar.offset` or `bar.belowMenuBar`, this step names it with a button to edit it: setup saves your choice as the default, but what the profile sets still applies over it, so the bar may not end up where you chose, even once **Continue** moves on. Presets, the clock and what the bar needs depend on this choice, so it comes first. On a short screen, a step that doesn't fit scrolls above **Continue**. **Skip setup** closes setup without saving.

The fourth step lists the permissions the bar you chose needs, each with **Allow…**: **Accessibility** always, because the app menus, the Apple menu, Menu bar items, Workspaces and window clearance depend on it; **Automation**, to let StableBar control System Events, when setup has changed or still needs to change the macOS menu bar setting; and **Calendars** when your bar has Meetings. Once macOS has asked, Automation and Calendars offer **Open Settings…** instead; Accessibility keeps **Allow…**, which then opens System Settings. Setup asks for nothing before this step, apart from Automation in the first: picking a preset with Meetings doesn't open the Calendars dialog. The live bar still works while setup is open, so clicking its Apple logo without Accessibility asks for it, as it does any time. Each turns to **Allowed** as soon as macOS allows it, and **Continue** waits until all of them have. **Keep the menu bar instead** drops the need for Automation. **Set up Meetings later** drops it for Calendars when only the preset's Meetings asks: a calendar widget already on your bar still needs Calendars. If one is still missing at the last step, it shows above **Finish setup**, which waits for it.

When your chosen bar has Workspaces on macOS Spaces, the permissions step also checks **Desktop shortcuts**. It shows how many desktops cannot be switched from the bar yet and **Open Keyboard Shortcuts…**; choose **Mission Control** there and enable **Switch to Desktop N** for each desktop you need. It shows **Ready** when all are on and names desktops beyond macOS’s shortcut range. This check never holds up **Continue** or **Finish setup**: you can turn the shortcuts on later in **Settings → General → Access**. It re-checks when you return to StableBar, open the page or add or remove a desktop, without polling or changing your shortcuts.

The second step offers presets under **Widgets** and themes under **Theme**. Each preset names its theme and shows its widgets as icons in bar order, with the ones it adds brighter. From the starter bar on the top edge, every preset ends the bar with Sound, Battery when it adds it, the clock and Menu bar items, in that order: **Minimal** keeps the app menus on Built-in; **Developer** adds Workspaces, System and Battery on Phosphor in Dark; **Workday** adds Meetings and Battery on Léman; **Notch** adds Battery on the Notch theme, and appears only while the bar is on the top edge and a connected display has a notch, since that black bar blends into the notch only there; **Everything** adds Workspaces, Meetings, System and Battery on Catppuccin. On a Mac without a battery, such as a Mac mini, presets leave Battery out and don't show its icon. Below the macOS menu bar, which already shows the Apple menu, the app's menus, the clock and its menu bar items, presets build on the starter with Workspaces, System and Sound instead, without a clock, and leave out Menu bar items. On a bar you have changed, a preset adds its widgets to yours. A preset chooses the [built-in widgets](widgets.md) to add, a theme and an appearance; pick another theme afterwards and the preset's widgets stay. Click the chosen preset again to keep your current bar. A chosen Notch preset stays available to deselect, even if the notched display disconnects or you move the bar below the menu bar.

Choosing another preset, or clicking the chosen one again, changes only its widgets, theme and appearance: your clock, window and notification clearance, **Set up Meetings later** and **Open at login** choices stay, and the clock and Meetings choices apply whenever the bar has those widgets.

Under **Theme**, a row of cards shows each [theme](themes.md) that ships with StableBar or that you have installed, drawn as a small bar. Click a card, or move through them with ← and →; the card marked **Current** keeps your theme as it is. Tab and Shift-Tab reach the placement, preset and theme tiles even with macOS Keyboard navigation off; the focused tile has a ring, and Space chooses it. VoiceOver reads each tile's name and selected state, with widget names for presets. **Light**, **Dark** and **Automatic**, beside the heading, control the bar's appearance, and the cards follow it; **Clock** next to them switches between 24-hour and 12-hour, and shows only while your bar has a clock. Setup shows your current appearance and clock, and **Keep current** leaves them untouched; the placement is always the first step's choice. The bar shows the effect of active profiles, and the last step names any profile that still controls a setting, each with a button to edit it. Setup saves your defaults and preserves custom widgets, scripts and imported files.

A new configuration places the bar on the top edge when the macOS menu bar hides automatically, and below the menu bar otherwise; existing configurations retain their position. Below the menu bar, each display's bar starts right under that display's own menu bar, whatever its height (`bar.belowMenuBar`). Earlier versions placed it there with a fixed `bar.offset` instead, which a taller menu bar partly covers; the first time StableBar starts while the menu bar shows, it turns such an offset into `"belowMenuBar": true` with an offset of 0, in your configuration and in each screen setup's own bar. Choosing **Replace the menu bar** in setup turns on **Automatically hide and show the menu bar**, so the menu bar no longer covers the bar; **Keep the menu bar** turns it off; later, [**Automatically hide the menu bar**](#hide-the-native-menu-bar) in Settings turns it on or off. Setup makes this change through System Events, so it first checks that StableBar may control System Events: the first time, macOS asks, and setup waits for your answer. Without that access, setup leaves the menu bar as it is and offers **Open Automation Settings…**; turn on **System Events** under StableBar in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation**, come back, and setup hides or shows the menu bar. Skipping or closing setup puts the menu bar setting back. Quitting StableBar leaves the setting as it is, but StableBar remembers when setup or Settings turned auto-hide on, so [Uninstall](#uninstall) turns it off again. Neither placement reserves space for windows. Setup's third step offers **Keep windows clear of StableBar** and **Keep notifications clear of StableBar**, and gives separate guidance when it detects AeroSpace, yabai or OmniWM, or when Workspaces uses macOS Spaces. See [Keeping windows clear of the bar](#keeping-windows-clear-of-the-bar). Its **Open at login** switch shows whether StableBar opens at login. Once you choose **Replace the menu bar** with StableBar in an Applications folder, the switch starts on, since macOS then keeps the menu bar hidden even while StableBar isn't running; turning it off keeps StableBar from opening at login. Outside an Applications folder, the step offers **Show in Finder** instead of the switch.

In **Finishing touches**, **Keep windows clear of StableBar** starts on when setup detects no window manager running, and off when it detects one running. This also applies when you run setup again. An explicit `bar.keepWindowsClear` value in your configuration, including an included file or an active profile, keeps its value. You can turn the switch off before **Finish setup**; choosing another preset keeps this default. It uses the Accessibility access setup already requires, and starts moving windows only once you finish. **Skip setup** saves nothing. Outside setup, omitting `bar.keepWindowsClear` still leaves window clearance off.

**System** shows CPU and memory use with details in its popup; **Meetings** shows your next meeting and today's agenda; **Battery** shows the charge with details in its popup; **Sound** shows the volume; [**Menu bar items**](widgets.md#menu-bar-items) lists the items in the macOS menu bar. These widgets use native providers and run no scripts.

**Workspaces** uses AeroSpace, yabai, OmniWM or Rift when one is installed and running: AeroSpace and yabai on your login shell's `PATH`, OmniWM as its app or `omniwmctl` on the `PATH`, Rift as `rift-cli` on the `PATH`. When setup picks the source, or you add a Workspaces widget, StableBar keeps the source your configuration names while that one is installed, and otherwise takes the first one installed and running, in that order; a running bar keeps its source and doesn't switch on its own. When the first launch finds none, for example because your login shell's `PATH` arrives late, its starter uses macOS Spaces. Setup looks for a manager once, when it opens: until a setup finishes, reopening setup looks again, as long as that starter is unchanged. yabai needs [its signals](widgets.md#yabai-workspaces), OmniWM needs [**Enable IPC**](widgets.md#omniwm-workspaces), and Rift needs [each Space turned on](widgets.md#rift-workspaces). Without any of them, Workspaces shows your [macOS Spaces](widgets.md#macos-spaces-workspaces), which switch desktops through the **Switch to Desktop** shortcuts and need Accessibility access to do it.

**Finishing touches** checks the connection when the previewed bar has yabai or OmniWM Workspaces. For yabai it offers the missing refresh signals to copy, or a reminder to load saved signals with `yabai --restart-service`. For OmniWM it asks you to open the app first if needed, then points to **Enable IPC** if the connection is unavailable. Choose **Check Again** after making the change; returning to StableBar checks again while that step is open, keeping the last result and any expanded signals visible. Slow or unavailable checks stay unverified. Checks stop when you leave the step and never run on a timer. You can finish setup with any of these results and follow the linked guide later.

A preset adds widgets but never removes any. A matching widget already on your bar stays as it is; a turned-off one is turned on with its settings kept. A different widget with the same ID is kept. Active profile overrides still apply.

The starter bar shows the Apple menu, the frontmost app's menus, Sound, the clock and Menu bar items. Below the menu bar, the starter shows Workspaces, System and Sound instead, since the menu bar right above it shows the rest: choosing **Keep the menu bar** in setup turns an unchanged starter into this one, and **Replace the menu bar** turns it back. On a bar you have changed, including with `widgetOverrides`, `widgetOrder` or a screen's `layout`, in the file or in a profile, setup keeps your widgets; below the menu bar it removes only unchanged copies of the starter's Apple menu, app menus, clock and Menu bar items, and none when your configuration uses `includes` or packages, whose files can refer to them. The menus need Accessibility access; until you allow it, a warning icon takes the app's menus' place, except while setup is open, and clicking the Apple logo asks for access. Setup's permissions step lists Accessibility, which clearance and menu widgets use, and Calendars when a calendar widget needs it, with their status and a permission action or System Settings link. The first **Allow…** for Accessibility, in setup or in **Settings → General → Access**, shows the macOS prompt; later clicks open **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility**. Status updates when you return from System Settings, or within a second for Accessibility, and the bar shows the app menus. A newly chosen Meetings widget stays off your bar during the tour until you allow Calendar access in the permissions step. Allow it, or choose **Set up Meetings later**, which saves that widget turned off so **Finish setup** doesn't wait for Calendars. Enable it later in **Bar**. Existing calendar widgets stay unchanged. Scripts that control other apps ask for Automation access when used; AeroSpace and yabai manage their own Accessibility permission.

Until **Finish setup**, your choices are temporary changes, like command-line changes without `--persist`: the bar shows them, but the file is unchanged. Window and notification clearance keep following your bar as it was when setup opened, including earlier unsaved command-line changes, so no window moves for a choice you might skip; they follow setup's choices, including where the bar goes, once you finish. **Skip setup** (Esc) puts your bar back as it was; once you have changed something, it first asks **Skip setup?**, where **Keep Setting Up** (Return or Esc) takes you back. Permission buttons act immediately through macOS. Open at login is off until enabled and requires the app to be in Applications. If macOS requires Login Items approval, setup shows the remaining action. Setup opens by itself at launch until you finish or skip it. After that, **Getting Started…** in the menu-bar icon's menu and **Settings → General → Help → Open setup…** open it. While a skipped setup was never finished, **Settings → General → Access** also shows **Finish setting up**.

## Download a release

Stable releases after v0.4.0 on the [releases page](https://github.com/Seb-GRAF/StableBar/releases) include `StableBar.dmg`. Betas of the next version are marked **Pre-release**. Both are built for Apple silicon. Releases signed with a Developer ID are notarized and open like any app. Earlier ones are ad-hoc signed, so macOS blocks them the first time you open them: allow it in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway**.

To install the latest stable release with [Homebrew](https://brew.sh) instead, run `brew install --cask seb-graf/tap/stablebar`.

## First launch

The first launch creates `~/.config/stablebar/config.json` from [Resources/config.json](../Resources/config.json), with the bar on the top edge, or below the menu bar when the menu bar doesn't hide, where it starts with Workspaces, System and Sound. Subsequent launches keep your file. If it is a symbolic link, Setup, Settings and command-line saves update the linked file and keep the link. Right-click the bar or use its macOS menu-bar icon to choose **Edit Bar in Place**, **Try Themes…**, **Settings…** or **Quit StableBar**. While editing, the menus offer **Finish Editing Bar**. **Settings → General** controls login, window and notification clearance, access and workspace connections, opens or reloads the configuration file, and links to updates, setup and help. Placement is in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**.

Keep personal settings in `~/.config/stablebar/config.json` and personal scripts in `~/.config/stablebar/widgets/`, outside the repository. Point commands directly at those scripts. `Resources/config.json` is the shared starter configuration; rebuilding the app does not overwrite your personal file. Local scripts should load any supporting files from their own directory.

StableBar sits at the top edge of each display, or, with `bar.belowMenuBar` (**Below the menu bar** in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**), right under each display's macOS menu bar; a display whose menu bar hides keeps the bar on its top edge. `bar.offset` moves the bar further down from there. It hides on native fullscreen Spaces. AeroSpace gaps are managed separately. Of the macOS menu-bar settings, StableBar changes only auto-hide, in setup and in [Settings](#hide-the-native-menu-bar). Keep the center clear on a notched display. The default configuration uses only the left and right groups.

## Updates

A release signed with a Developer ID checks GitHub for a newer version shortly after launch and every four minutes. These silent checks pause while an update check, offer, download or installation is already active, and stop making requests when automatic checks are off. A failed check retries at the next interval. Sparkle still chooses the compatible release and handles its download and installation. As soon as a check finds one, a filled circular download icon with a small theme-accent dot fades in at the right end of the bar and moves the neighboring widgets aside. Reduce Motion disables that animation. Click it to see the changes from every release published after your installed version through the offered version, grouped newest first. The popup loads this history from GitHub, including releases no longer in the update feed. Its height stays at 420 points, with scrolling release notes and the download action kept below them. If the history cannot be loaded, it shows the offered release's notes and a link to all changes on GitHub. **Download** checks again and downloads the newest compatible release on your update track, so an older pending offer does not require an intermediate installation. Then choose **Install and Relaunch** once it's downloaded. Updates already downloaded keep their install action. The bar button stays icon-only and changes to a filled circular sync icon when the update is ready; its tooltip describes the current stage. Download and preparation progress appear inside the popup without increasing its height. Seven days after the first launch of a release that includes it, the popup also shows **Enjoying StableBar? Star it on GitHub** above the download button. Clicking it opens the GitHub repository, where you can star StableBar yourself, and × dismisses it; either choice hides it for good. **Settings → General → Help → Star on GitHub** keeps the link. **Check for Updates…** in the menu-bar icon's menu or in **Settings → General → Updates** checks right away and remains available while an update waits. **Settings → General → Updates** also turns off automatic checks, or lets StableBar download updates and install them when it quits.

**Update track** chooses the releases you get. **Stable** gets stable releases. **Beta** gets betas of the next version and stable releases, whichever is newer. An installed beta starts on Beta. Switching back to Stable keeps your beta until a newer stable release is out, because StableBar never installs an older version.

Beta version names include a UTC timestamp and build number, such as `0.5.0-beta.20261020103000.114`, to keep new betas in order on GitHub. In-app updates compare the build number.

Only an app signed with a Developer ID updates itself, because the update must be signed by the same developer as the app it replaces. Ad-hoc signed releases and the builds you make yourself don't check for updates; **Settings → General → Updates** links to the releases page instead.

## Delay the native menu bar

When the macOS menu bar hides automatically, **Settings → General → Native menu bar → Reveal delay** sets how long to hold the pointer at the top edge before it appears. Enter milliseconds: `1000` is one second, `2000` is two, and `3000` is three. `0`, the default, adds no delay. Allow StableBar in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility**.

StableBar holds mouse movement six points below each display's top edge until the delay expires, out of reach of the band where macOS 27 reveals the menu bar by itself. Moving away cancels the wait. Drag events pass through. This works while menu-bar auto-hide is enabled and StableBar is running; turning the delay off or quitting restores normal pointer behavior. The wait is added before macOS's own reveal animation.

Set `bar.menuBarRevealDelay` in JSON, including in an included file or a profile. See the [menu-bar delay example](../examples/menu-bar-delay/config.json):

```json
{ "bar": { "menuBarRevealDelay": 2000 } }
```

## Hide the native menu bar

**Settings → General → Native menu bar → Automatically hide the menu bar** turns macOS's **Automatically hide and show the menu bar** on or off, as setup's **Replace the menu bar** and **Keep the menu bar** do. Turn it on when the bar sits on the top edge in the menu bar's place, and off to show the menu bar again, for example after turning on **Below the menu bar** in **Settings → Bar → Bar → Placement**. It changes the macOS setting through System Events, so it stays after StableBar quits. The first time, macOS asks to let StableBar control System Events; without that access, the toggle stays as it is and **Open Settings…** opens **Privacy & Security → Automation**, where you turn on **System Events** under StableBar. Back in StableBar, the change applies. **System Settings → Menu Bar**, or **Control Center** before macOS 26, shows the same setting. [Uninstall](#uninstall) turns it off when StableBar turned it on.

On macOS 27, while the menu bar hides automatically, a display with a notch shows the menu bar when you click where it would be. StableBar keeps its bar above that area there, so clicks reach the bar, and steps under the menu bar while macOS shows it.

**Settings → General → Native menu bar → Always hide** keeps the macOS menu bar invisible while StableBar runs, even when the pointer reaches the top edge. Quitting StableBar shows it again. In JSON, set `"bar": { "hideMenuBar": true }`.

Without it, the menu bar shows on hover as usual. StableBar makes it invisible only while its [Control Center and Menu bar items](widgets.md#menu-bar-items) widgets open an item, and shows it again once the item closes.

## Keeping windows clear of the bar

macOS provides no public API for a custom bar to reserve screen space. Without a tiling window manager, enable **Settings → General → Windows and notifications → Keep windows clear of the bar** (`bar.keepWindowsClear`, off by default; setup turns it on when no window manager runs) and allow StableBar in **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility**. StableBar then moves overlapping ordinary windows below the bar and reduces their height when needed to keep them above the Dock. It follows each display's bar height, offset and scale, and waits until you finish dragging before adjusting a window. See [the window clearance example](../examples/window-clearance/config.json).

Native fullscreen windows, minimized windows and dialogs are left alone. Some apps do not expose movable or resizable windows, or enforce a minimum size that cannot fit; StableBar cannot keep those clear. Disabling the option stops adjustments and leaves windows where they are. Revoking Accessibility access pauses adjustments; granting it resumes them. A locally rebuilt, ad-hoc signed app may need to be authorized again.

Leave this option off when another window manager controls your gaps. With AeroSpace, set `gaps.outer.top` in `~/.aerospace.toml` to the bar's panel height (40 by default), plus any `bar.offset` beyond the menu bar; AeroSpace accepts per-monitor values. yabai users can set `external_bar` with the same clearance. OmniWM measures `gaps.outer.top` in its `settings.toml` from the display's top edge and subtracts the menu bar itself, so set it to the panel height plus `bar.offset`. With Rift, set `top` under `[settings.layout.gaps.outer]` in `~/.config/rift/config.toml`. A below-bar underline adds its maximum configured gap plus thickness beyond the existing bottom margin. For example, a 38 pt bar with no margin, a 2 pt underline and a 3 pt gap needs 43 pt. With display scale `s`, round upward to whole screen points: `max(ceil(height × s), ceil(height × s − margin + maximum gap × s) + ceil(maximum thickness × s))`. StableBar reserves this maximum across states, so focus or mode changes never move windows or resize panels.

On macOS 26, enable **Settings → General → Windows and notifications → Keep notifications clear of the bar** (`bar.keepNotificationsClear`, off by default) to move notification banners 16 points below the bar on the display that shows them. It uses the same Accessibility access and can stay on with AeroSpace, yabai or OmniWM. The Notification Center panel and desktop widgets stay where macOS puts them. This depends on Notification Center's Accessibility structure and does not support macOS 14 or 15.

## Uninstall

**Settings → General → Uninstall → Uninstall…** removes StableBar and undoes what it changed on your Mac; search Settings for "uninstall" to find it. Its confirmation lists each step, then StableBar:

- quits any other StableBar that's running. If one doesn't quit, Uninstall stops before changing anything;
- turns off the macOS menu bar's auto-hide when setup or Settings turned it on. Auto-hide that StableBar didn't turn on, for example because you hid the menu bar before installing it, stays on unless you check **Turn off the macOS menu bar's auto-hide**. Setups finished before Uninstall existed didn't record this, so check it if setup hid your menu bar. Auto-hide that setup turned off stays off;
- turns off **Open at login**;
- removes the `stablebar` command that [**Command line**](command-line.md#install-the-command) installed, asking for an administrator password when its folder needs one. A `stablebar` command that StableBar didn't write stays;
- deletes package and source tokens from the Keychain;
- quits. Once it has quit, it resets its Accessibility, Calendars and Automation permissions, deletes its caches and preferences and moves StableBar to the Trash. These steps wait so the bar doesn't keep running without its permissions or its app.

**Also move ~/.config/stablebar to the Trash** is off by default, because that folder holds your configuration, scripts and packages. A configuration elsewhere, opened with `--config`, stays. If a step fails, for example because you cancel the password prompt, the other steps still run. StableBar lists what's left to do by hand and stays whole until you click **Quit StableBar**, which finishes the rest, unless another StableBar kept running: then quit that one and uninstall again. If StableBar can't start the steps that wait for it to quit, it lists them too, to do by hand. If a step fails after StableBar has quit, an alert lists what's left to do by hand.

When Homebrew installed StableBar, the app stays in Applications: run **Uninstall** first and click **Quit StableBar**, then run `brew uninstall --cask stablebar`. `brew uninstall --zap --cask stablebar` does both: it runs `stablebar --uninstall --yes`, then also deletes `~/.config/stablebar`.

`stablebar --uninstall` does the same from Terminal; see [Command-line controls](command-line.md).

If StableBar is already gone, undo its changes by hand:

1. Turn off **Automatically hide and show the menu bar** in **System Settings → Menu Bar**, or **Control Center** before macOS 26.
2. Remove StableBar from **System Settings → General → Login Items** and from **Accessibility**, **Calendars** and **Automation** in **System Settings → Privacy & Security**.
3. Run `sudo rm /usr/local/bin/stablebar` if StableBar installed that command, and `security delete-generic-password -s StableBar` until it finds no item.
4. Delete `~/.config/stablebar`, `~/Library/Caches/org.stablebar.app`, `~/Library/Caches/org.stablebar.app.sparkle`, `~/Library/Caches/StableBar`, `~/Library/HTTPStorages/org.stablebar.app` and `~/Library/Preferences/org.stablebar.app.plist`.
